The Story

50,000 years in the making.

The only gemstone first found in a meteorite. Here's how a speck of stardust became the most brilliant stone you can wear.

50,000 BC

A star falls

A meteorite strikes the Arizona desert, carving out the Canyon Diablo crater — and carrying microscopic crystals with it.

1893

A discovery

Chemist Henri Moissan finds tiny, brilliant crystals in crater fragments. At first he believes they're diamonds.

1904

A new gem

The crystals turn out to be silicon carbide — a new mineral, named moissanite in his honour.

Today

Perfected

Natural moissanite is too rare to wear, so ours is lab-grown: flawless, conflict-free, and cut for maximum fire.

Questions

Good to know.

Is moissanite a real gemstone?

Yes. Moissanite is a naturally occurring mineral (silicon carbide). Because natural moissanite is extraordinarily rare, the moissanite used in jewelry is lab-grown — chemically and optically identical to the natural crystal.

Does it look like a diamond?

To most eyes, yes — it's colourless and extremely brilliant. Up close, you may notice more rainbow "fire" than a diamond gives off. Many people consider that a feature.

Will it pass a diamond tester?

Most basic thermal diamond testers read moissanite as diamond because both conduct heat very well. Dedicated moissanite testers can tell them apart.

How long does shipping take?

Each piece is made to order and hand-set, then shipped insured with tracking. Standard delivery takes 7–14 business days; express is 4–7 business days. You'll get tracking by email as soon as it ships.

How do I find my ring size?

Wrap a strip of paper around the base of your finger, mark where it overlaps, and measure the length in millimetres. Circumference of 49 mm ≈ size 5, 52 mm ≈ size 6, 54 mm ≈ size 7, 57 mm ≈ size 8, 59 mm ≈ size 9.

How do I care for it?

Moissanite is extremely durable. Clean it with warm water, mild soap and a soft toothbrush to keep the sparkle at its brightest. Remove sterling silver pieces before swimming.
